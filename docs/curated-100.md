# The Superflix 100

A curated shortlist of 100 Super Nintendo / Super Famicom games, compiled
October 2026 from published best-of lists, hardware histories, and the
fan-translation scene. The full SNES library is 1,749 official releases
(977 of them Japan-only); this page is the editorial answer to "what do I
actually add to the catalog first?"

| Tier | Count | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [The canon](#part-1--the-canon-163) | 63 | Consensus classics from ranked editorial lists |
| [Unusual & historically interesting](#part-2--unusual--historically-interesting-6475) | 12 | Hardware milestones, distribution oddities, rarities |
| [Japan-only with English fan translations](#part-3--japan-only-playable-in-english-via-fan-translation-76100) | 25 | The deep catalog that never left Japan, now playable in English |

## Method

- **Canon tier** — starts from two complete ranked editorial lists, IGN's
  *Top 100 SNES Games* (2011) and GamesRadar+'s *The 25 best SNES games of
  all time* (updated March 2026), cross-checked against Wikipedia's
  cross-publication aggregation of "best games of all time" lists (games
  included on at least six publications' lists). Titles were selected for
  consensus strength and genre coverage, not raw list position.
- **Unusual / historical tier** — hardware storylines (Super FX chip,
  Super Scope, SNES Mouse), distribution oddities (EU-only, NA-only,
  cancelled-then-finished), original-IP cult classics, and extreme
  rarities, verified against Wikipedia's hardware and release-list
  articles.
- **Japan-only tier** — titles with **complete English fan
  translations**, drawn from GameRant's roundup and the *SNES Fan
  Translations Collection* index, with each translation verified against
  its Romhacking.net patch page (linked per entry). No partial or
  machine translations. Patches only — this project never bundles or
  links ROM images.

Release years, developers, publishers, and region claims were checked
against Wikipedia's master list of SNES releases. Years are the original
Japanese retail year unless the North American release came first (noted
where it matters).

## Part 1 — The canon (1–63)

1. **The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past** (1991, Nintendo EAD) —
   IGN #1 · GR #1 · ★. The Light/Dark dual-world adventure that
   effectively wrote the template for 2D action-adventure.
2. **Chrono Trigger** (1995, Square) — IGN #2 · GR #2 · ★. The
   Sakaguchi/Horii/Toriyama "dream team" RPG; New Game+ and multiple
   endings became genre conventions.
3. **Super Metroid** (1994, Nintendo R&D1 & Intelligent Systems) —
   IGN #3 · GR #3 · ★. The atmospheric, map-driven blueprint of the
   Metroidvania and a masterclass in wordless storytelling.
4. **Final Fantasy VI** (1994, Square; released as *Final Fantasy III*
   in NA) — IGN #4 · GR #9 · ★. Ensemble cast, the opera set-piece, and
   a mid-game apocalypse that reset expectations for JRPG plotting.
5. **Super Mario World** (1990, Nintendo EAD) — IGN #5 · GR #4 · ★.
   Packed-in launch game that introduced Yoshi and a secret-exit-filled
   Dinosaur Land; the console's best seller at 20.6 million units.
6. **Super Street Fighter II Turbo** (1994, Capcom) — IGN #6 · GR #5.
   The definitive 16-bit fighter: super combos, all sixteen characters,
   and the speed setting the series kept.
7. **Super Mario World 2: Yoshi's Island** (1995, Nintendo EAD) —
   IGN #7 · GR #6 · ★. Storybook crayon art over a Super FX 2 sprite
   engine; platform design that never stopped inventing.
8. **Super Mario Kart** (1992, Nintendo EAD) — IGN #8 · GR #8 · ★.
   Invented the kart-racing genre — Mode 7 tracks, items, battle mode.
9. **Star Fox** (1993, Nintendo EAD & Argonaut) — IGN #9 · GR #21 · ★.
   The SNES's first 3D polygon game, built to show off the Super FX chip.
10. **Super Mario RPG: Legend of the Seven Stars** (1996, Square) —
    IGN #10. The Nintendo × Square crossover with isometric, timing-hit
    combat; Geno and Mallow remain fan favorites.
11. **Secret of Mana** (1993, Square) — IGN #11 · GR #10 · ★. Ring-menu
    action RPG with genuine three-player co-op via the Super Multitap.
12. **Mega Man X** (1993, Capcom) — IGN #12 · GR #15 · ★. Dashing,
    wall-jumping reinvention of Mega Man with a surprisingly dark story.
13. **EarthBound** (1994, Ape & HAL) — IGN #13 · GR #7 · ★.
    Suburban-Americana JRPG; a famous marketing flop ("this game
    stinks!") that grew into one of gaming's great cult classics.
14. **Final Fantasy IV** (1991, Square; released as *Final Fantasy II*
    in NA) — IGN #14 · ★. Introduced the Active Time Battle system that
    defined a decade of JRPGs.
15. **Tetris Attack** (1995 JP as *Panel de Pon*, 1996 NA, Intelligent
    Systems) — IGN #15 · GR #12. A famous localization swap — the fairy
    cast became Yoshi's Island characters — that launched the Puzzle
    League series.
16. **Donkey Kong Country 2: Diddy's Kong Quest** (1995, Rare) —
    IGN #16. Rare's 2D platforming peak, carried by David Wise's
    soundtrack and a punishing second-quest coin hunt.
17. **Super Punch-Out!!** (1994, Nintendo) — IGN #17 · GR #14.
    Pattern-reading boxing with huge, expressive opponents.
18. **F-Zero** (1990, Nintendo EAD) — IGN #18 · GR #16. Mode 7 launch
    showpiece; futuristic racing leagues above anything the NES could do.
19. **ActRaiser** (1990, Quintet) — IGN #19 · GR #23. God-game city
    building interleaved with side-scrolling action; still nothing else
    like it.
20. **Super Mario All-Stars** (1993, Nintendo EAD) — IGN #20. The NES
    *Mario* games remade in 16-bit on one cartridge.
21. **Super Castlevania IV** (1991, Konami) — IGN #21 · GR #11 · ★.
    Eight-direction whipping plus Mode 7 showpieces — the rotating
    clock-tower room is still a technical flex.
22. **Mario Paint** (1992, Nintendo EAD) — IGN #22. The SNES Mouse
    pack-in: fly-swatting mini-games and a remarkably deep music
    composer.
23. **The Magical Quest Starring Mickey Mouse** (1992, Capcom) —
    IGN #23. Gorgeous Disney platformer built around costume abilities
    (firefighter, wizard, armor).
24. **Contra III: The Alien Wars** (1992, Konami) — IGN #24 · GR #18 · ★.
    Series peak: two-weapon carry, clinging to guided missiles, top-down
    interlude stages.
25. **Mortal Kombat II** (1994, Acclaim) — IGN #25. Arrived after the
    SNES censorship of the first game had helped ignite the US ratings
    debate — blood intact this time.
26. **Donkey Kong Country** (1994, Rare) — IGN #26 · GR #17 · ★. The
    pre-rendered "ACM" graphics sensation that reshaped 16-bit-era
    marketing.
27. **Wario's Woods** (1994, Nintendo & Intelligent Systems) — IGN #29.
    Matching-action puzzler against Wario; also the last game Nintendo
    licensed on the NES.
28. **The Lost Vikings** (1993, Silicon & Synapse) — IGN #30. Three
    switchable Vikings, one escape puzzle at a time — from the studio
    that became Blizzard.
29. **Mega Man X2** (1995, Capcom) — IGN #31. Wireframe pseudo-3D boss
    fights powered by the Capcom Cx4 enhancement chip.
30. **The Legend of the Mystical Ninja** (1991, Konami; first *Ganbare
    Goemon* released in the West) — IGN #32. Platformer stages fused
    with explorable Edo towns; absurd, and proud of it.
31. **Metal Warriors** (1995, LucasArts & Konami) — IGN #33. Climb-in,
    bail-out mech combat — and a North America exclusive.
32. **Lufia II: Rise of the Sinistrals** (1995, Neverland) — IGN #34.
    Puzzle-dungeon design years ahead of its time, plus the Ancient Cave
    roguelike sidequest.
33. **SimCity** (1991, port by Nintendo EAD & Intelligent Systems) —
    IGN #35. The Japanese-developed launch port, complete with Bowser as
    a disaster monster.
34. **NBA Jam** (1994, Iguana Entertainment / Acclaim) — IGN #36 · GR #20
    (Tournament Edition). "He's on fire!" — the arcade 2-on-2 that owned
    the era.
35. **U.N. Squadron** (1991, Capcom) — IGN #37. *Area 88* side-scrolling
    shooter with money management between sorties.
36. **Kirby's Dream Course** (1994, HAL & Nintendo EAD) — IGN #38.
    Kirby as a golf-ball physics puzzle game; quietly one of Nintendo's
    strangest spin-offs.
37. **Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles IV: Turtles in Time** (1992, Konami)
    — IGN #39 · GR #24. Best-in-class arcade brawler conversion, from
    "Big Apple, 3 AM" to "Bury My Shell at Wounded Knee."
38. **Earthworm Jim 2** (1995, Shiny) — IGN #40. Cow launches, bagpipe
    races — a parody platformer with real craft.
39. **Ogre Battle: The March of the Black Queen** (1993, Quest) —
    IGN #41. Yasumi Matsuno's real-time tactical RPG with a reputation
    system and branching paths.
40. **E.V.O.: Search for Eden** (1992, Almanic) — IGN #42. Evolve a
    creature across geological eras; an action RPG about Darwinism.
41. **Breath of Fire** (1993, Capcom) — IGN #43. Dragon-morphing RPG
    that started Capcom's JRPG line.
42. **Demon's Crest** (1994, Capcom) — IGN #44. Firebrand (*Gargoyle's
    Quest*) in a dark, non-linear Metroidvania.
43. **Gradius III** (1990, Konami) — IGN #45. A Japanese launch title
    and the series' hardest hour — early proof of the SNES's arcade
    chops.
44. **Harvest Moon** (1996, Pack-In-Video) — IGN #46. Founded the
    farm/life-simulation genre.
45. **Kirby Super Star** (1996, HAL) — IGN #47 · GR #19. An
    eight-games-in-one anthology with the drop-in "helper" co-op
    mechanic.
46. **Zombies Ate My Neighbors** (1993, LucasArts) — IGN #48. B-movie
    co-op run-and-gun — chainsaws, weed whackers, 3D glasses.
47. **Super Bomberman** (1993, Hudson Soft) — IGN #49. Introduced the
    four-player Super Multitap; SNES party gaming starts here.
48. **Super Ghouls 'n Ghosts** (1991, Capcom) — IGN #51 · GR #25. The
    double-playthrough difficulty gauntlet, newly armed with a
    double jump.
49. **Breath of Fire II** (1994, Capcom) — IGN #53. Cult follow-up
    with a famously rough localization.
50. **Axelay** (1992, Konami) — IGN #55. Mode 7 "into-the-screen"
    pseudo-3D shooter stages; a Konami technical flex.
51. **Earthworm Jim** (1994, Shiny) — IGN #57. "Groovy!" — the era's
    most expressive animation in a platformer.
52. **Donkey Kong Country 3: Dixie Kong's Double Trouble!** (1996,
    Rare) — IGN #58. The trilogy's under-loved finale, with the best
    puzzle design of the three.
53. **Disney's Aladdin** (1993, Capcom) — IGN #60. Capcom's platformer,
    entirely distinct from Virgin's Genesis game — the famous case of
    two studios, two Aladdins.
54. **Kirby's Dream Land 3** (1997 NA, HAL; not released in Japan
    until 1998) — IGN #62. Pastel Kawaii Kirby with animal friends; the
    quiet close of the SNES Kirby era.
55. **International Superstar Soccer** (1994 JP as *Perfect Eleven*,
    1995 NA, Konami) — IGN #64. The series that made Konami the 16-bit
    football benchmark.
56. **R-Type III: The Third Lightning** (1993, Irem) — IGN #65. The
    Force-pod formula perfected with the Cyclone pod trio.
57. **Mega Man X3** (1995, Capcom) — IGN #67. The X trilogy's close;
    Zero steps in as a semi-playable ally.
58. **Pocky & Rocky 2** (1994, Natsume) — IGN #73. Cute-em-up
    run-and-gun sequel with recruitable partner characters.
59. **Illusion of Gaia** (1993, Quintet) — IGN #74. The middle chapter
    of the "Quintet trilogy"; a globe-trotting action RPG with
    transformation powers.
60. **Soul Blazer** (1992, Quintet) — IGN #76. Rebuild a world
    creature-by-creature as you free souls from monster lairs.
61. **Shadowrun** (1993, Beam Software) — IGN #77. Isometric cyberpunk
    RPG-adventure where the Matrix runs in wireframe.
62. **Flashback** (1993, Delphine Software) — IGN #79. Rotoscoped
    cinematic platformer with an amnesiac agent on the run.
63. **Pilotwings** (1990, Nintendo EAD) — IGN #80 · GR #22. The
    launch-day flight school that put Mode 7 scaling and rotation on
    the map — biplane, rocket belt, hang glider.

## Part 2 — Unusual & historically interesting (64–75)

64. **Star Fox 2** (developed 1995, Nintendo EAD & Argonaut; released
    2017) — Finished but cancelled as the Nintendo 64 era began, then
    completed and officially released on the Super NES Classic in 2017
    with its free-range flight mode intact. The best argument that the
    Super FX 2 story didn't end where history says it did.
    ([Star Fox 2 — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Fox_2))
65. **Doom** (1995, Sculptured Software) — id's shooter squeezed onto a
    Super FX 2 cartridge; famously capped at a smaller viewport and
    enemies facing the player, still an engineering landmark. In 2025
    it even returned as a physical cartridge with an in-cart "Super FX
    3" running on a Raspberry Pi RP2350.
    ([List of Doom ports — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Doom_ports))
66. **Wolfenstein 3D** (1994, Imagineer) — A Japanese-developed console
    port of the Nazi-shooting FPS, with blood turned to sweat and guard
    dogs replaced with giant rats: Nintendo's censorship era in a single
    cartridge.
    ([Wolfenstein 3D — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfenstein_3D))
67. **Super Scope 6** (1992, Nintendo R&D1 & Intelligent Systems) — The
    pack-in for the rocket-shaped Super Scope bazooka light gun; six
    mini-games that made the case for a peripheral with a famously short
    life.
    ([Super Scope 6 — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Scope_6))
68. **Yoshi's Safari** (1993, Nintendo R&D1) — The Mario franchise's
    only light-gun rail shooter: Mario rides Yoshi through Bowser's
    jewel-powered invasion. It exists, and it's playable today.
    ([Yoshi's Safari — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshi%27s_Safari))
69. **Terranigma** (1995, Quintet) — The finale of the Quintet
    action-RPG line, in which you resurrect continents — released in
    Japan and Europe but never North America. A top candidate for "best
    SNES game most Americans never got to buy."
    ([Terranigma — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terranigma))
70. **Super Turrican 2** (1995, Factor 5) — The German studio's
    run-and-gun showpiece with a grappling arm and Mode 7 vehicle
    stages; never released in Japan. Resurfaced in 2022 as a Strictly
    Limited Games "Special Edition."
    ([Super Turrican 2 — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Turrican_2))
71. **Plok** (1993, Software Creations) — Ste and John Pickford's
    limb-throwing original IP, owned by nobody's licensing department,
    with a legendary Tim Follin soundtrack. The cult platformer the
    Pickfords spent decades wishing had a sequel.
    ([Plok — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plok))
72. **Wild Guns** (1994, Natsume) — Wild West × science fiction gallery
    shooter with a dodge-roll and lasso; a cult hit whose rarity
    earned it a 2022 retro re-release from Natsume Atari itself.
    ([Wild Guns — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_Guns))
73. **Rendering Ranger: R²** (1995, Rainbow Arts) — Manfred Trenz's
    run-and-gun/shmup hybrid. The PAL version was cancelled weeks from
    release, leaving a tiny Japanese-only print run — one of the
    rarest, most expensive SNES cartridges in existence.
    ([Rendering Ranger: R² — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendering_Ranger:_R%C2%B2))
74. **Uniracers** (1994, DMA Design) — Unicycle stunt racing from the
    studio that became Rockstar North; pulled from shelves after a
    trademark complaint over the unicycle design. One of the SNES's
    fastest, strangest racers.
    ([Uniracers — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniracers))
75. **Rock n' Roll Racing** (1993, Silicon & Synapse) — Vehicular
    combat racing with vocals on the soundtrack, from Blizzard before
    it was Blizzard (IGN #72). The missing link between
    *Rock n' Roll Racing* and *Rockstar* is one shared studio.

## Part 3 — Japan-only, playable in English via fan translation (76–100)

All 25 received complete English fan-translation patches. Patch links go
to Romhacking.net's database entry — patch files only, applied to a ROM
you own. See [Using this list with Superflix](#using-this-list-with-superflix).

### Role-playing games

76. **Final Fantasy V** (*Final Fantasy V*) (1992, Square) — The job
    system pinnacle Square skipped in the West. Translation: RPGe,
    October 1997 — the first major SNES fan translation, the project
    that effectively founded the scene.
    [Patch #353](https://www.romhacking.net/translations/353/)
77. **Dragon Quest V: Hand of the Heavenly Bride** (*Tenkū no Hanayome*)
    (1992, Chunsoft/Enix) — A generation-spanning tragedy where you can
    recruit monsters; its NA version (*Dragon Warrior V*) was announced
    and cancelled. Translation: byuu, 2001.
    [Patch #342](https://www.romhacking.net/translations/342/)
78. **Dragon Quest VI: Realms of Revelation** (*Maboroshi no Daichi*)
    (1995, Heartbeat/Enix) — The dream/reality dual-world finale of the
    Zenithian trilogy. Translation: NoPrgress, 2001.
    [Patch #344](https://www.romhacking.net/translations/344/)
79. **Seiken Densetsu 3** (*Seiken Densetsu 3*; the NA version was
    cancelled as *Secret of Mana 2*) (1995, Square) — Three-protagonist,
    branching action RPG; Neill Corlett's patch was the scene's first
    mega-hit in 2000. Officially localized only decades later as
    *Trials of Mana*. Translation: LNF/Corlett/SoM2Freak, 2000.
    [Patch #440](https://www.romhacking.net/translations/440/)
80. **Tales of Phantasia** (*Tales of Phantasia*) (1995, Wolf
    Team/Namco) — The first *Tales* game: side-scrolling real-time
    combat and a staggeringly produced cartridge. Translation: DeJap,
    2001 (famous for its liberties — see *Legends of Localization*).
    [Patch #470](https://www.romhacking.net/translations/470/)
81. **Star Ocean** (*Star Ocean*) (1996, tri-Ace/Enix) — Late-gen
    showpiece with voice acting and an overflow chips-and-all
    presentation; the *Star Ocean* series starts here. Translation:
    DeJap, 2003.
    [Patch #452](https://www.romhacking.net/translations/452/)
82. **Live A Live** (*Live A Live*) (1994, Square) — Eight
    self-contained chapters across seven eras that converge in a
    finale; *Octopath Traveler*'s concept, 24 years earlier.
    Translation: Aeon Genesis, 2008.
    [Patch #381](https://www.romhacking.net/translations/381/)
83. **Treasure of the Rudras** (*Rudra no Hihō*) (1996, Square) — Spell
    creation by combining word-fragments, and four parallel parties on
    a 16-day countdown. Translation: Aeon Genesis.
    [Patch #669](https://www.romhacking.net/translations/669/)
84. **Romancing SaGa 3** (*Romancing SaGa 3*) (1995, Square) — Eight
    protagonists, non-linear open-world structure, and the series'
    signature openness about failure. Translation: Mana Sword, 2000.
    [Patch #416](https://www.romhacking.net/translations/416/)
85. **Shin Megami Tensei** (*Shin Megami Tensei*) (1992, Atlus) — The
    first mainline SMT: post-apocalyptic Tokyo, demon negotiation and
    fusion. The sequels *SMT II* (1994) and *SMT if...* (1994) are also
    fully translated. Translation: Aeon Genesis.
    ([SMT — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shin_Megami_Tensei_(video_game)))
86. **Bahamut Lagoon** (*Bahamut Lagoon*) (1996, Square) — Strategy RPG
    where you raise dragons and position them on grid battlefields.
    Translation: DeJap, 2002; a retranslation with bugfixes arrived in
    2023 ([patch #5938](https://www.romhacking.net/translations/5938/)).
    [Original patch #280](https://www.romhacking.net/translations/280/)

87. **Tengai Makyō Zero / Far East of Eden Zero** (*Tengai Makyō Zero*)
    (1995, Red Company/Hudson) — A sprawling comedic RPG that used the
    console's real-time clock to trigger events; the *Far East of Eden*
    series' missing masterpiece. Translation: Dynamic-Designs (Tom),
    v2.0 in 2018. [Patch #3243](https://www.romhacking.net/translations/3243/)

### Action, adventure, and beyond

    [Patch #490](https://www.romhacking.net/translations/490/)

## Using this list with Superflix

- **Import priority** — work through Part 1 first when building a real
  catalog; the games are common, well-documented, and match No-Intro
  names cleanly. Then use Part 3 to give the catalog a deep library
  nobody else serves.
- **Japan-only entries** — import patched copies as `NTSC-J` `roms`
  variants of the same `games` record. Apply the Romhacking.net patch
  to a headerless No-Intro dump with a tool like FLIPS or
  [Rom Patcher JS](https://www.romhacking.net/patch/). A patched image
  no longer matches the No-Intro DAT hashes, so store the hashes of the
  *original* ROM on the record and note the patch source (group, year,
  patch URL) in the description.
- **Satellaview titles** (Radical Dreamers) run as ordinary ROMs in the
  Snes9x core; no special handling is needed.
- **Legal hygiene** — this page deliberately links only to patch
  databases and documentation. Never commit ROMs, patches, box art, or
  DAT-derived assets to this repository (see the project rules).
